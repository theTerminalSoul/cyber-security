2. Primary Role
	Bits Transmission: Converts raw data (1s and 0s) into physical signals (electrical, optical, or radio) and transmits them across physical media.

	Hardware Interface: Deals directly with the physical hardware, cables, connectors, and wireless transmitters/receivers.

2. Key Functions
	Signal Encoding: Translates binary data into electrical voltages, light pulses, or radio waves.

	Data Rate Control: Defines the transmission speed (bit rate), establishing how many bits are sent per second.

	Synchronization: Aligns the clock timings between the sender and receiver so they stay in sync.

	Physical Topologies & Modes: Defines how devices are physically connected (bus, star, mesh) and the direction of data flow (simplex, half-duplex, or full-duplex).

3. Key Components & Standards
	Media Types: Twisted-pair copper cables (e.g., Cat6), fiber-optic cables, and wireless media (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth).
	Connectors & Hardware: RJ45 connectors, network interface cards (NICs), hubs, and repeaters.

The Physical Layer doesn't care what the data means—it only cares about moving raw 1s and 0s over wires or air.
